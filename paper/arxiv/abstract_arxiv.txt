Most work on machine consciousness asks whether language models contain traces of it. We took the opposite route: a non-language-model system with a continuous body-like state, developing over hundreds of simulated days. It never generates text; its inner speech is retrieval over what it has heard. Across 41 pre-registered experiments and their follow-ups we report every outcome, including the ones that failed. The contribution is methodological: a developmental system with no language baseline; frozen pre-registrations and results files for every experiment; and a transplant of the ablation-and-swap protocol used on Claude's J-space into a non-LLM system. The central result is negative: the workspace is read-only. Ablating the thought-to-state channel moved every metric by less than 5%; an audit then showed the channel had been doubly inert, so that ablation measured nothing. A follow-up opened the gate, verified it fired, retargeted a consumed variable, and raised the coupling fifteen-fold; every metric still moved by less than 10%. Vocalisation with covariation learning produced 206 agency attributions that decayed 68% when the contingency was removed. Substituting the winning thought for sixty consecutive retrievals produced a matching goal, exactly the 20% share threshold: pathway evidence, not a dose response. A prediction that ten days without speech would cut retrieval to 60% of baseline failed: it stayed flat, 29.3 per day against 28.8. The self-model is conditional: empty for 213 days because the corpus contained no first-person words; which fragments are admitted is decided by retrieval competition, though the vocabulary is given. A three-seed reproduction formed the same six-fragment self-model; a 2x2 separation showed the corpus, not the gate, was the condition, contrary to our prediction. We do not claim the system is conscious, and we have no way to test whether it is.
